Sites for new housing near stations in Copenhagen (Example)

Sites for new housing near stations in Copenhagen (Example)

Properties1
tags—

Nov 26, 20266 min read

A worked case in strategic planning: where could Copenhagen place new housing? Method: Site screening by criteria (Task); concepts in Multi-criteria analysis. Compare with Densification priorities in Copenhagen by multi-criteria analysis (Example), which weighs criteria instead of using them as filters.

Københavns Kommune’s population forecast calls for some 40,000 new dwellings during the current twelve-year municipal plan period. This example asks which parcels could take some of them, by filtering the city’s parcels through a few conditions: close to a station, no protected building, little built today, not a park, and enough land left. 1,260 parcels pass all five filters, with 1,101 ha between them. The filters do what the data let them do, and the largest site they find is a lake.

1. Spatial Intent

Question: which parcels in Københavns Kommune could take new housing, if new housing should be close to rail transport, spare the city’s heritage and parks, and go where the land is used lightly today?

Units of observation: cadastral parcels.

Result: a map of the parcels that pass, and at least one map of the criteria and data that selected them.

What the data cannot say:

  • Addresses are not dwellings. An office building and a block of flats can both have one address; m² per address measures how many addresses share the land, not how densely people live.
  • No address is not no building. 1,741 of the parcels that pass the density filter have no address at all: schools, utilities, port land and water.
  • Water is not a layer. The parks layer covers parks, not lakes, harbour basins or railway land. Nothing excludes them.
  • SAVE covers the buildings it covers. 29,770 buildings have the value −1 and 3,559 have 0: not assessed, which is not the same as not worth preserving.

600 m is the walking distance of the Fingerplan’s station proximity principle, which is written for offices; it is borrowed here for housing. The other thresholds are this example’s own choices. With the parcel’s centre point (point on surface) inside the buffer instead of intersect, step 2 keeps 20,968 parcels instead of 21,747.

4. The result

The median site is 0.29 ha; half lie between 0.15 and 0.69 ha. The few largest hold much of the area:

The largest is the lake Damhussøen: its parcel lies within 600 m of a station, has no address and no building, and is not in the parks layer, so every filter lets it through. It shows what step 6 of the Task asks for: look at the largest results before trusting the rest.

A rough check of scale. 40,000 dwellings of 89 m² (the mean size of dwellings built in Copenhagen since 2000, see Dwelling size by construction period in Copenhagen (Example)) need some 3.6 million m² of floor area. On 1,101 ha that is 0.32 m² of new floor area per m² of land, before roads, schools and green space.

5. Points for the discussion

  • Which filter does the work? Station proximity removes 42% of the parcels, protection another 30% of what is left, density 61% of the rest. A different density threshold moves the result more than any other choice.
  • Every filter is a veto. A parcel 610 m from a station is out, however light its use. The multi-criteria example lets criteria compensate each other instead.
  • Missing criteria. Water, railway land, cemeteries, contaminated soil, noise from roads and railways (the railway layer is in the database but not used), existing local plans (spatial planning decisions): each would remove sites that pass here.
  • Light use is not free land. Many parcels with much land per address are schools, sports grounds, allotment gardens or industry. Building on them moves a use, it does not fill an empty space.
  • Parcels follow ownership. A large parcel passes or fails as a whole; the part near the station and the part far from it are not told apart.

Compare the two examples

Run Densification priorities in Copenhagen by multi-criteria analysis (Example) on the same parcels and compare the two selections: how many of its top 100 parcels pass this screening, and which of the screening’s sites does the weighting rank low? Where the two methods agree, the result does not depend on the method; where they disagree, the disagreement is the discussion.
