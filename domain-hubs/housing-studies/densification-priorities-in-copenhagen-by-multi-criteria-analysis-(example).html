Densification priorities in Copenhagen by multi-criteria analysis (Example)

Densification priorities in Copenhagen by multi-criteria analysis (Example)

Properties1
tags—

Nov 26, 20269 min read

A worked case in strategic planning: which parts of Copenhagen should be densified first? Method: Multi-criteria suitability (Task), with Scoring criteria (Task); concepts in Multi-criteria analysis. Compare with Sites for new housing near stations in Copenhagen (Example), which uses the criteria as filters instead.

Densification can mean more dwellings or more floor area in total. This example ranks every parcel in Københavns Kommune by five criteria at once, weighted in two scenarios, and maps the top 100 of each. The data come with each criterion already rescaled to 0–100, which raises the question of the scale itself: should the criteria have been rescaled differently, for example as z-scores? With the scores as given, the weights hardly decide anything. 117 parcels where the free-space score has no meaning, roads and parcels without an address, fill 99 of the top 100 places as if they had the most room to build. Once they are set to NULL, the criterion with the widest spread outweighs the others whatever weights are chosen. Rescaling as percentile ranks restores the weights, but then the rule for empty values decides.

Example data

The course file martrikler_multi_kreterier.gpkg (EPSG:25832): 33,818 parcels with five criteria, each as a raw value and as a score from 0 to 100. It is not yet published as a snapshot package.

1. Spatial Intent

Question: which kvarterer should Copenhagen prioritise for densification, under two different sets of priorities?

Units of observation: cadastral parcels, ranked. Units of presentation: the top 100 parcels of each scenario, read by kvarter.

Scenarios used here:

CriterionScenario 1: the transit cityScenario 2: heritage and green space
Distance to station−0.4−0.1
Distance to Rådhuspladsen−0.20
Free space+0.2+0.2
SAVE value+0.2+0.4
Distance to green areas0−0.3

A negative weight because a high distance score is bad for densification; positive for free space (much land per address) and for low preservation value.

2. Auditing the data

Before any weighting, the distributions of the scores:

The free-space score measures how much land each address has, and so how much room a parcel has for more dwellings. It has two problems.

  • One extreme value. One parcel has 3,339,865 m² per address, so dividing by the maximum leaves every other parcel close to 0: the median score is 0.01.
  • 117 parcels score 100, the most free space possible, where the measure has no meaning. For 57 parcels the whole area is road (regareal = vejareal): there is no land to build on, and the 0 m² per address was scored as 100. 60 parcels have no address, so the division is by zero; they include the 33 parcels of Assistens Kirkegård (18.5 ha, all inside the parks layer) and the lake Damhussøen.

For these 117 parcels the free-space score is set to NULL: it is undefined, not high. A weighted sum with a NULL is NULL, so they get no suitability and no rank; 33,701 parcels are ranked. Left at 100, they fill 99 of the top 100 in both scenarios.

Empty SAVE values. 8,365 parcels have no SAVE value: no building on them was assessed. They score 100, the same as the lowest preservation value. That treats “not assessed” as “nothing worth preserving”.

SAVE is ordinal. Its classes 1–9 are ranks, not measurements; dividing by 9 treats each step as equal.

Settings used

  • Audit: Field calculator s_kvm = if("regareal" = "vejareal" OR "adresser" IS NULL, NULL, "score_kvm_pr_adresse"): 117 NULLs.
  • Rescale (percentile rank, per criterion): Add autoincremental field r_station, sort "score_afstand_til_station" ascending, NULLs last; then Field calculator p_station = if("score_afstand_til_station" IS NULL, NULL, 100 * mean("r_station", group_by:="score_afstand_til_station") / count("score_afstand_til_station")). The same for the other criteria, with s_kvm for free space. count("x") counts only the values that are not NULL.
  • Weight: Field calculator suit_s1 = -0.4*"p_station" - 0.2*"p_centre" + 0.2*"p_kvm" + 0.2*"p_save", and suit_s2 with the weights of scenario 2.
  • Rank: Add autoincremental field rank_s1, sort "suit_s1" descending, NULLs last; Extract by expression "rank_s1" <= 100.

4. Divide by the maximum, z-score or percentile rank?

The same weights, three ways of rescaling:

The effective weight is the nominal weight times the score’s standard deviation, as a share of the total (see the Task, step 7).

  • As given, the 117 parcels scored 100 take the top: any positive weight on free space puts them first, and the two scenarios become the same map.
  • As given, after the audit, free space has lost its influence (a standard deviation of 0.6 against 26.5 for SAVE). Scenario 2 is three-quarters SAVE, whatever its weights say. Scenario 1’s top 100 lie in Frederiksstaden (20), Metropolzonen (17), Christianshavn Neden Vandet (15) and Vesterbro øst (12): central kvarterer. All 100 parcels have no SAVE value, and therefore score as if nothing on them were worth keeping.
  • z-scores give every criterion a standard deviation of 1, so the weights would mean what they say. But extreme values stay extreme: the parcel with 3.3 million m² per address gets z = 175 on free space, and a suitability of 34 in scenario 1, against 4.8 for the next. z-scores answer the question of spread, not of outliers.
  • Percentile ranks make the effective weights equal to the nominal ones, are not moved by extreme values, and suit the ordinal SAVE classes. What they give up are the distances: the parcel 20 m from a station and the one 200 m away can be neighbours in rank.

For this exercise percentile ranks are the most defensible choice, with the audit before them. The rest of this example uses them. They do not solve the empty SAVE values, see below.

The transit city’s top 100 lie a median 159 m from a station and 3.1 km from Rådhuspladsen; those of heritage and green space lie a median 8 m from a green area and 4.3 km from the centre. Both favour large parcels, because a large parcel has much land per address.

The empty SAVE values decide both scenarios. 98 of scenario 1’s top 100 and all of scenario 2’s have no SAVE value. A quarter of the parcels (24.5%) share the score 100, which as a percentile rank is 88: the same as the few parcels with SAVE 9, and higher than any other assessed parcel can reach. Weighting SAVE therefore mostly selects parcels without assessed buildings. A different rule, such as giving them the median, or leaving SAVE out and screening listed and high-value buildings first, gives other maps. Try one and compare.

6. Points for the discussion

  • The scale is a weight. Before any weight is set, the rescaling has already decided how much each criterion counts. Report effective weights next to the nominal ones.
  • Compensation. A parcel next to a station can make up for a listed building on it. Where a criterion should be a veto, screen first, as in Sites for new housing near stations in Copenhagen (Example), and weigh what is left.
  • Missing is a value. “No SAVE value” and “no address” both scored 100, and here that rule decides the result more than any weight does. Each rule for missing values is a choice that can move parcels to the top.
  • Free space is not empty space. Kolonihavekvarteret enters scenario 1’s top: allotment gardens have much land per address. Densifying them removes a green use the green-space criterion does not see.
  • Ranks are relative. The top 100 are the best of Copenhagen’s parcels under these weights, not good sites in themselves.

Compare the two examples

The screening in Sites for new housing near stations in Copenhagen (Example) and this ranking ask the same question of mostly the same parcels. Of the 1,260 sites the screening finds, 797 can be matched by jordstykke_id to this file. Of the top 100 parcels in scenario 1, 76 pass the screening; in scenario 2, 42 do. Compare the maps: where both methods point, the result does not depend on the method; where they disagree, ask which criteria were treated as vetoes in one and as trade-offs in the other.
